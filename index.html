🔬 Investigational peptide explainer

B7-33: a simplified relaxin, aimed at scarred tissue

B7-33 is a lab-built, single-chain peptide modeled on a fragment of the human hormone relaxin-2. In animal studies it shows anti-scarring (anti-fibrotic) signals — but it has never been tested in people and is not a medicine anywhere. This page maps what it is, how it's thought to work, and exactly where the evidence stops.

🧪

Strictly preclinical — zero human trials

Everything here comes from laboratory and animal research. There are no human safety data, no approved uses, and no established way to take B7-33. Animal findings do not translate directly to people.

01

What B7-33 is

A trimmed-down copy of a natural hormone — built for the lab, not the pharmacy.

B7-33 is a synthetic single-chain peptide — a deliberately simplified version of human relaxin-2 (also called H2 relaxin), a natural hormone involved in tissue remodeling and blood-vessel function.

Researchers built it by taking part of relaxin's B-chain and re-engineering it into a shorter, linear (straight-line) molecule that is far easier to synthesize than the full hormone. Preclinical concept

It is studied mainly in laboratory and animal models for potential anti-fibrotic (anti-scarring) effects — and it is not an approved medication in any country.

The one-sentence version: B7-33 is "relaxin, distilled" — the fragment researchers cared about, rebuilt as one simple chain for easier production and study.

Simplified by design · two chains become one

Native relaxin-2 is a two-chain, folded hormone. B7-33 keeps only the B-chain portion researchers wanted, rebuilt as one straight chain. Conceptual explanation

02

The design idea

Why rebuild a hormone instead of just using it?

Natural relaxin-2 is biologically interesting but structurally awkward: two chains, folded and cross-linked — expensive and fiddly to produce, and harder to study cleanly.

B7-33 is the answer to a design question: what is the smallest piece that might keep the useful biology? By isolating the B-chain segment and straightening it into a linear peptide, researchers got a molecule that is simpler to synthesize and simpler to test — while aiming to preserve the receptor signaling they cared about. Mechanistic interpretation

Relaxin-2 (two chains)→ keep B-chain fragment→ B7-33 (one linear chain)
The bet being tested: a trimmed, easier-to-make peptide that keeps the anti-fibrotic signaling of the parent hormone — without the full molecule's complexity.
03

Biased signaling at RXFP1

Not just "on" — selectively on. This is the core pharmacology idea.

Both relaxin and B7-33 act through RXFP1 (relaxin family peptide receptor 1). The native hormone switches on several downstream pathways at once — useful, but broad.

B7-33 is described as a biased agonist: it preferentially drives the ERK1/2 pathway, which raises production of enzymes such as MMP-2 that break down excess collagen — while only weakly triggering the cAMP pathway. Mechanistic interpretation

The hypothesis: anti-fibrotic payoff with fewer of the broader effects seen with full relaxin. Selective signal, narrower ripples.

Why "biased" matters: receptors are like switchboards with multiple outgoing lines. A biased agonist doesn't just flip the master switch — it favors one line over the others, aiming for the desired outcome with less collateral signaling.

One receptor, two lines · the bias

B7-33 is thought to favor the ERK1/2 line (collagen-clearing enzymes up) while barely nudging cAMP — the selectivity behind its anti-fibrotic hypothesis. Mechanistic interpretation

04

What animal research explored

Scarred tissue and struggling organs — all in animal models.

Preclinical work has centered on conditions involving excess tissue scarring and organ dysfunction:

  • Heart: less fibrosis in cardiac models, smaller damage after experimental heart injury, and better measures of heart function. Animal study
  • Lungs: reduced scarring in pulmonary fibrosis models. Animal study
  • Kidneys: less fibrosis in renal models. Animal study
  • Blood vessels: protective signals on vascular function. Animal study
  • Pregnancy models: early exploration in models of pregnancy complications involving blood-vessel problems. Animal study
Read the badges, not just the bullets: every finding above sits at the animal-study evidence tier. Promising directions — not proof of anything in humans.

How fast did anything change?

Animal timelines only — there is no human clock.

In animal models, shifts in tissue fibrosis markers and organ-function readouts appeared over days to weeks of administration. Some studies reported less collagen buildup and better heart or lung measures within one to several weeks. Animal study

No human timeline exists — because no human trials have been completed. Any "how long until it works" estimate for people would be pure speculation.

06

Safety: knowns and unknowns

The honest version: mostly unknowns.

No formal human safety data exist. Everything below comes from animal work and theoretical reasoning — not from people taking B7-33.

💉Local injection-site reactions were noted occasionally in preclinical work. Animal study
🩸Transient blood-pressure effects are theoretically possible, since the peptide acts on blood vessels. Not measured in humans — a mechanistic caution, not an observed event. Mechanistic interpretation
❓Immune reactions and long-term effects in humans remain unknown. There is simply no data. Conceptual explanation

Serious adverse events have not been systematically reported in the available animal literature at the doses used for anti-fibrotic research — but with zero human trials, human risk cannot be assessed with confidence, and researchers stress that animal findings don't transfer directly to people.

A note on the wilder claims. Online discussions sometimes describe personal experiments with B7-33 or expected benefits for scarring and heart health. These accounts are entirely anecdotal — unsupported by any human clinical evidence — and should never be read as medical guidance. Anecdotal

Who it may interest — and who should steer clear

A research-community topic, not a consumer one.

🔬 May interest

  • Fibrosis researchers studying scarred heart, lung, or kidney tissue
  • Clinicians tracking heart-failure and anti-fibrotic science
  • People curious about experimental peptides for organ scarring or cardiovascular support — as a science topic

Interest here is scientific: reading papers, following trials that don't yet exist, understanding the pharmacology.

🚫 Should steer clear

  • Anyone seeking an approved therapy — this isn't one
  • Pregnant or breastfeeding individuals
  • People with low blood pressure or conditions sensitive to vasodilation
  • Those with known peptide allergies
  • Children, the elderly, and people with complex conditions — unstudied
  • Anyone outside a controlled research setting

With no human data, there is no safe-use profile to speak of.

Bottom line: B7-33 is not appropriate for self-use or as a treatment outside formal research protocols.
08

How researchers give it

What lab protocols did — not instructions.

In research settings, B7-33 has been administered to animals mainly by subcutaneous injection or other parenteral (non-oral) routes. Laboratory protocols typically ran daily or continuous delivery over short periods. Animal study

There is no established human administration method, schedule, or formulation. The above describes what researchers did to animals — it is a protocol fact, not guidance, and nothing here should be read as instructions.

B7-33 terminology

Ten terms, each with its scientific meaning and a plain-English mirror. Tap each term.

Scientific MeaningB7-33 is a synthetic single-chain peptide engineered as a simplified analogue of human relaxin-2. It was derived from a portion of the hormone's B-chain and restructured into a shorter linear peptide that is easier to synthesize. It functions experimentally as a biased agonist at the relaxin family peptide receptor 1 (RXFP1) and is investigated in preclinical models for anti-fibrotic activity. It has no approved medical use and has not been evaluated in humans.
Plain EnglishB7-33 is a lab-made, single-strand peptide designed as a stripped-down copy of the natural hormone relaxin-2. Scientists took part of the hormone's B-chain and reshaped it into a shorter, straight molecule that is much simpler to manufacture. In experiments it acts as a selective switch for the relaxin receptor and is studied in animals for its potential to reduce scarring. It is not a medicine, has no approved uses, and has never been tested in people.
Scientific MeaningRelaxin-2 is an endogenous peptide hormone of the insulin/relaxin superfamily, composed of two chains (A and B) joined by disulfide bonds. It signals primarily through RXFP1 and participates in tissue remodeling, collagen turnover, and vascular regulation, with prominent roles in pregnancy physiology. Its complex two-chain structure makes it comparatively difficult to manufacture, which motivated the development of simplified analogues such as B7-33.
Plain EnglishRelaxin-2 is a natural hormone made of two linked chains, best known for its role in pregnancy and in remodeling tissues and blood vessels throughout the body. It works mainly through the RXFP1 receptor, influencing how collagen is built up and broken down. Because its two-chain folded shape is hard and expensive to produce, researchers created simpler stand-ins like B7-33 that try to keep the useful signaling with far less structural complexity.
Scientific MeaningRXFP1 (relaxin family peptide receptor 1) is a G protein-coupled receptor that serves as the primary signaling receptor for relaxin-2. Upon activation it can engage multiple intracellular cascades, including ERK1/2 phosphorylation and cAMP production, depending on the ligand and cellular context. It is the molecular target through which both native relaxin and B7-33 are thought to exert their effects, and the site at which B7-33's signaling bias is expressed.
Plain EnglishRXFP1 is the main docking station on cells that relaxin-2 plugs into — and the same receptor B7-33 is designed to activate. Once switched on, it can trigger several different internal chain reactions, including the ERK1/2 and cAMP pathways. Which of those reactions dominates depends on what exactly binds the receptor, which is why a "biased" molecule like B7-33 can favor one outcome over another through this single receptor.
Scientific MeaningA biased agonist is a ligand that, upon binding its receptor, preferentially activates one downstream signaling pathway over others coupled to the same receptor. This contrasts with a balanced (unbiased) agonist, which engages the receptor's pathways in their native proportions. Biased agonism is pursued therapeutically on the premise that favoring a beneficial pathway while minimizing others may improve the benefit-to-side-effect profile. B7-33 is characterized as biased toward ERK1/2 signaling relative to cAMP at RXFP1.
Plain EnglishA biased agonist is a molecule that turns a receptor on selectively — pushing one of its internal signaling routes much harder than the others, instead of activating them all evenly the way the natural hormone would. Drug designers like this idea because it might deliver the wanted effect while dodging unwanted ones tied to the other routes. B7-33 is described as biased toward the ERK1/2 route at the relaxin receptor, with only weak action on the cAMP route.
Scientific MeaningERK1/2 (extracellular signal-regulated kinases 1 and 2) are central kinases in the MAPK signaling cascade that relay receptor activation into changes in gene expression and cell behavior. In the context of RXFP1 signaling, ERK1/2 activation is linked to increased expression of matrix metalloproteinases, including MMP-2, which degrade extracellular collagen. B7-33's preferential engagement of this cascade is the proposed mechanistic basis for its anti-fibrotic effects.
Plain EnglishERK1/2 is a key internal signaling chain that carries a receptor's message deeper into the cell, changing which genes get switched on. When the relaxin receptor activates this chain, the cell ramps up production of collagen-dissolving enzymes like MMP-2. B7-33's preference for this exact chain is the leading explanation for why it might reduce scarring: more signal here means more of the enzymes that clear away excess collagen.
Scientific MeaningMMP-2 (matrix metalloproteinase-2, also called gelatinase A) is a zinc-dependent enzyme that degrades components of the extracellular matrix, including collagen. It is a key effector of tissue remodeling: controlled MMP activity clears excess or damaged matrix, while dysregulated activity contributes to disease. Increased MMP-2 expression downstream of ERK1/2 signaling is one proposed mechanism by which B7-33 could reduce fibrotic collagen deposition in animal models.
Plain EnglishMMP-2 is an enzyme that chews up the structural scaffolding between cells, including collagen — the main ingredient of scar tissue. It is one of the body's natural remodeling tools: in the right amount it clears away excess or damaged tissue framework. B7-33 is thought to raise MMP-2 levels through the ERK1/2 pathway, which is how it might dissolve the extra collagen that builds up in fibrotic organs.
Scientific MeaningCyclic AMP (cAMP) is a ubiquitous intracellular second messenger produced when certain G protein-coupled receptors activate adenylyl cyclase. It regulates a wide array of cellular processes, from metabolism to vascular tone. Native relaxin robustly stimulates cAMP production via RXFP1, contributing to its broad physiological footprint. B7-33 activates this pathway only weakly, and that relative sparing is central to its characterization as a biased agonist.
Plain EnglishcAMP is one of the cell's most common internal messengers — a chemical relay involved in everything from energy use to how blood vessels behave. Natural relaxin triggers a lot of cAMP signaling through its receptor, which is part of why the hormone's effects are so wide-ranging. B7-33 only weakly activates this messenger system, and that restraint is exactly what makes it "biased": strong on the ERK1/2 route, quiet on the cAMP route.
Scientific MeaningFibrosis is the pathological accumulation of excess extracellular matrix — predominantly collagen — in a tissue or organ, typically following chronic injury or inflammation. It stiffens tissue, disrupts normal architecture, and progressively impairs organ function in the heart, lungs, liver, and kidneys. Because fibrosis represents collagen deposited faster than it is cleared, strategies that increase matrix-degrading enzyme activity, such as the MMP-2 upregulation proposed for B7-33, are investigated as anti-fibrotic approaches.
Plain EnglishFibrosis is harmful scarring inside organs: after repeated injury or long-term inflammation, the body lays down too much collagen and the tissue stiffens, slowly choking off normal function in the heart, lungs, liver, or kidneys. Since scarring is essentially collagen piling up faster than it gets cleared, one treatment strategy is to boost the enzymes that dissolve it — which is the rationale behind B7-33's proposed anti-fibrotic action.
Scientific MeaningPreclinical research comprises all investigation conducted before human testing: experiments in cell cultures, tissue samples, and animal models. It establishes basic pharmacology, plausible mechanisms, and initial safety signals, and it is a required precursor to clinical trials — but it cannot establish human efficacy or safety. B7-33 exists entirely at this stage: every finding attributed to it comes from laboratory or animal work, with no clinical (human) data whatsoever.
Plain EnglishPreclinical means "before human testing" — all the work done in lab dishes and animal models to figure out how a compound behaves and whether it's worth pursuing further. It is a necessary first step, but it can never prove something works or is safe in people. B7-33 lives entirely in this zone: everything known about it comes from cells and animals, with zero human studies behind it.
Scientific MeaningVasodilation is the widening of blood vessels through relaxation of vascular smooth muscle, which lowers vascular resistance and can reduce blood pressure. Relaxin signaling through RXFP1 influences vascular tone, which is why blood-pressure effects are considered a theoretical possibility with relaxin-mimetic peptides such as B7-33. In the absence of human data, this remains a mechanistic caution rather than an observed adverse event.
Plain EnglishVasodilation is the widening of blood vessels — the vessel walls relax, blood flows more easily, and blood pressure can dip. Because relaxin signaling naturally affects how vessels behave, drugs that mimic it could theoretically nudge blood pressure downward. For B7-33 this is a "worth watching" caution based on the biology, not something actually seen in people, since no human studies exist.

Takeaways — and the limits

What to carry away, and what this page can't give you.

🧬Simplified by design. B7-33 is a single-chain fragment of relaxin-2's B-chain, rebuilt linear for easier production — an engineered shortcut, not the hormone itself.
🎯Biased, not broad. It's thought to favor ERK1/2 signaling (collagen-clearing enzymes up) over cAMP at the RXFP1 receptor — selectivity is the whole thesis.
🫀Animal signals, three organs. Less fibrosis in heart, lung, and kidney models, plus heart-injury and vascular findings — all preclinical.
🚫Zero human data. No trials, no safety profile, no approved use, no established way to take it. The evidence ladder's third rung is empty.
🔬Research-only. Interesting to fibrosis and cardiovascular science; not appropriate for self-use or treatment outside formal protocols.
